# Noizes Market Research — Evidence Base

*Compiled July 2026. All figures sourced from public reports; links at each claim.*

Framing question: **who already pays for the problems Noizes solves?** (authenticity, provenance, collectability, permanent ownership, limited editions.)

---

## 1. Market size — the money that already flows

### The recorded-music baseline
- Global recorded music: **$31.7B in 2025**, up 6.4% — 11th consecutive year of growth ([IFPI Global Music Report 2026](https://www.ifpi.org/global-music-report-2026-global-recorded-music-revenues-grow-6-4-as-record-companies-drive-innovation/)).
- **Physical grew 8.0% in 2025 — outpacing digital for only the second time on record.** Vinyl up 13.7%, its 19th consecutive year of growth ([IFPI](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/global-recorded-music-revenues-hit-31-7bn-in-2025-up-6-4-yoy-users-of-paid-music-subscriptions-reach-837m/)).
- US: $11.5B total; physical $1.38B, of which **vinyl alone passed $1.04B (46.8M units)** ([RIAA 2025 year-end](https://www.riaa.com/riaa-reports-us-recorded-music-annual-revenue-achieves-new-high-of-11-5-billion-in-2025/)).

### The superfan opportunity (Noizes' actual TAM)
- Goldman Sachs "Music in the Air": superfan monetization is a **$4.3B/year incremental opportunity by 2026, ~$6.6B by 2035**, assuming ~20% of listeners are superfans willing to pay ~2x average ([MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/emerging-markets-superfans-and-price-rises-7-takeaways-from-goldman-sachs-new-music-in-the-air-report/), [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/korihale/2025/09/12/goldman-sachs-turns-up-superfans--emerging-markets-to-reach-200-billion-in-music-revenues/)).
- Luminate: US superfans = ~15% of the population; spend **$39/month on physical music (105% above average)** and $113/month on live; **73% buy merch** vs 26% of general listeners ([Luminate](https://luminatedata.com/blog/why-are-super-fans-so-valuable/), [MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/15-of-the-general-population-of-the-us-are-superfans-heres-what-that-means-for-the-music-business1/)).
- **Afropop/Afrobeats fans in the US spend 121% more on music per month than the average US listener** — directly relevant to a Kenya-rooted platform: the monetizable audience for East African artists is substantially the diaspora, paying in USD ([Luminate](https://luminatedata.com/blog/why-are-super-fans-so-valuable/)).

### Direct-to-fan money (the flow Noizes taps)
- Bandcamp: **$222M paid to artists in the last year alone**; $1.76B lifetime; Bandcamp Fridays hit a record $19M in 2025 ([Bandcamp](https://bandcamp.com/about), [MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/bandcamp-fridays-hit-154m-in-payouts-since-2020-with-19m-paid-in-2025-alone/)).
- Patreon: $2B/year to creators; **musicians are the top-earning category (~2–3x average, ~$1,100/month for serious creators)**; follower→patron conversion is typically 1–5% ([Patreon stats](https://electroiq.com/stats/patreon-statistics/)).
- Broader collectibles context: global collectibles market ~**$300–500B**, trading cards alone $13B+ and the fastest-growing segment ([Grand View](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/collectibles-market-report), [Market Decipher](https://www.marketdecipher.com/report/collectibles-market)). Music barely exists as a digital-collectible category — the gap the .nz format targets.

### ⚠️ The counter-signal you must not ignore
- **US paid digital downloads: ~$250M in 2025 and still declining** (digital albums fell 13.7% in H1) ([RIAA via Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/pro/riaa-2025-music-report-revenue-streaming-vinyl/), [CMU](https://completemusicupdate.com/cd-sales-in-the-us-plunge-in-first-half-of-2025-as-paid-streaming-subscriptions-hit-105-million-users/)).
- Read together with vinyl at $1.04B: **people do not pay for digital files; they pay for objects, scarcity, support, and identity.** An .nz package must be sold as a collectible edition/object, never as "a better download."

## 2. Competitor landscape

### The graveyard (what not to be)
| Platform | What it was | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Royal.io | Fractional royalty NFTs | Shut down late 2024 — trading volume collapsed, regulatory pressure |
| OneOf | $63M "green" music NFT marketplace | Collapsed |
| Sound.xyz | Music NFT drops → pivoted to subscription | Went offline Dec 2025 |
| **Nina Protocol** | Non-speculative, artist-keeps-100%, files on Arweave, critically loved | **Announced shutdown May 2026** |

Root cause per post-mortems: *"not enough actual fans willing to pay for the product, only speculators willing to flip it"* ([Chartlex post-mortem](https://www.chartlex.com/blog/business/music-nft-web3-post-mortem-2026)). Nina is the most sobering case — it had the right ethics, no crypto-speculation smell, 100% artist revenue, permanent files… and still couldn't sustain a business ([Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/music/articles/independent-music-platform-nina-protocol-230350268.html), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nina_Protocol)).

### The winners (what's working now)
- **EVEN** — sell-before-streaming storefronts. 500k+ artists via distributor deals (Too Lost's 400k, Symphonic), a **multi-year Universal Music Group D2C deal**, fans paying **avg $20+/release**, daily payouts, ~20% platform fee. Case study: an artist with 2,000 followers earned $700 from 23 superfans — ≈200k streams' worth ([EVEN](https://backstage.even.biz/), [MBW/UMG](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/universal-music-strikes-deal-with-superfan-app-even-to-power-d2c-sales-for-artists-worldwide/), [DMN](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2025/09/24/too-lost-even-superfan-app-onboard/)).
- **Weverse (HYBE)** — proof fans buy *digital* goods at scale when framed as fandom, not files: digital product purchases **doubled YoY**, 25.2M products sold in 2025, digital lines growing 30%/yr and driving the platform to profitability; 11–12M MAU ([MBW](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/hybes-us-restructure-weverse-turns-profitable-and-concerts-boom-3-things-to-know-from-the-k-pop-giants-latest-earnings-call/)).
- **Bandcamp** — still the D2C giant ($222M/yr) but visibly wounded: 50% staff cut after the 2023 Songtradr acquisition, and another round in June 2026 reportedly removed "most of the remaining engineers" ([Variety](https://variety.com/2023/music/news/bandcamps-layoffs-songtradr-1235758123/), [Tunefountain](https://www.tunefountain.com/bandcamp-engineering-layoffs/)). **Artists are actively worried about platform death — this is Noizes' single clearest wedge: a signed .nz file the fan owns outlives any store.**
- **Vault.fm** — built by the ex-Sound.xyz team, pivoted fully away from crypto to chat + early access + premium membership. Their pivot direction (access/connection, not tokens) is itself market evidence ([vault.fm](https://vault.fm/about)).

### Pattern across winners vs. losers
Winners sell **the moment and the relationship** (early access, exclusivity, closeness, fandom identity). Losers sold **the asset** (tokens, fractional rights, speculation). Noizes sells an authenticated object — it must attach that object to moments and identity (release-day editions, numbered pressings, provenance as bragging rights), or it pattern-matches to the graveyard.

### Where variant mania validates the model
Physical variant collecting is now industry-standard superfan monetization: Taylor Swift's *The Life of a Showgirl* shipped **34 variants**; Travis Scott's *Utopia* had 31; individual fans spend $800+ on one album's editions; collector psychology is set-completion ([Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/style/2025/12/03/album-variants-alternate-versions-special-editions/)). 62% of Gen Z buy vinyl to support artists, 56% for aesthetics ([musictalkers](https://musictalkers.com/latest-news/10159-how-taylor-swift-and-gen-z-are-driving-vinyl-and-cd-sales-growth-in-2026)). **A .nz edition is a variant with zero manufacturing cost, no shipping, and no pressing-plant queue** — the digital variant slot in a strategy labels already run.

## 3. Willingness to pay — what the numbers say

| Evidence | Price point |
|---|---|
| EVEN average per release | $20+ |
| Luminate superfan physical spend | $39/month |
| Patreon music patron pledge | $5–10/month |
| Vinyl LP (typical) | $25–45 |
| Deluxe box sets / signed editions | $75–300 |
| Variant collectors, single album | up to $800 |
| Weverse digital products | doubled YoY in volume |
| Plain digital downloads | ~$10 — **declining market** |

Working hypothesis for pricing: a standard .nz release should not price like a download (~$10) — it should price like a *digital pressing*: **$15–40 standard limited edition, $50–150 signed/numbered artist editions**, with edition size doing the scarcity work.

## 5. Beachhead & adoption strategy

The evidence supports the hypothesized beachhead — **independent artists with dedicated fanbases (≈5k–100k followers), for releases that matter** — with three sharpened edges:

1. **Position .nz as the digital variant / digital pressing, not a new marketplace.** Labels and artists already run variant strategies; Noizes slots into an existing behavior with existing budgets. "Limited digital pressing, signed and numbered" is language collectors already understand.
2. **Bandcamp instability is the acquisition moment.** The D2C incumbent is shedding engineers and trust in mid-2026. Noizes' pitch — *the file is signed, verifiable, and yours even if every server dies* — is the direct answer to the fear artists are voicing right now. There is a window here.
3. **For the Kenya angle, sell to the diaspora, not the local streaming market.** African streaming is ~$493M and mostly free/ad-supported ([madsolutions](https://madsolutions.ai/streaming-platforms-africa)); local willingness to pay for digital is low. But US-based Afropop/Afrobeats fans out-spend the average listener by 121%. Kenyan artists selling authenticated collectible editions to diaspora superfans in USD is a differentiated wedge no competitor is running.

### What the standards ambition requires
The PDF/EPUB comparison holds, but standards win via a killer app plus free reading: PDF won because Acrobat Reader was free and ubiquitous. Implication: **the .nz player/verifier must be free and frictionless everywhere; monetize the marketplace, signing, and publishing side.** A format nobody can open is not a standard.

### Sequencing implied by the evidence
1. Nail one flow: artist publishes a limited, signed .nz edition → fan buys in the release window (EVEN's sell-before-streaming model proves the window) → fan can verify/flex ownership.
2. Recruit via distributors and Bandcamp-refugee messaging, not one artist at a time.
3. Only after repeat purchases exist: resale/secondary market (provenance history becomes valuable only once primary demand is real — Royal died doing this backwards).
4. Institutional/archival market: real but slow; treat as credibility narrative, not revenue, for now.

## Biggest risks (unchanged by optimism)

1. **The graveyard is full of well-funded, well-intentioned versions of this.** Nina had permanence + 100% artist cut and died. Funding and ideology don't create demand; release-moment fandom does.
2. **"NFT smell."** Any positioning that reads as tokens/speculation inherits the corpse's reputation. The words that work: edition, pressing, signed, numbered, archive. The words that kill: token, mint, drop (arguably), on-chain.
3. **Digital-file demand is genuinely weak** ($250M and shrinking). The product must borrow demand from collecting (strong) and artist-support (strong), not from downloads (dead).
4. **Two-sided cold start.** EVEN solved it with distributor deals; Noizes needs an equivalent wholesale channel, not retail artist acquisition.
