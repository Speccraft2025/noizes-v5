# World asset bible

The Threshold world uses Edwardian domestic proportions, slightly compressed like a museum diorama but never cartooned. Door height is 2.1 m; the camera eye is 1.55 m; steps, table, windows and hallway derive from that reference.

Materials are soot-green plaster, walnut, faded botanical paper, oxidized brass, imperfect glass, bone paper and threadbare black cloth. Wear gathers at edges, handles, floor paths and water routes. Texture scale remains architectural: wallpaper motifs are hand-sized, floorboards foot-width and plaster cracks smaller than the key.

Geometry is low-poly but authored: large silhouette and spatial continuity take priority over hidden detail. Repeated vegetation and dust are instanced or point-based. No prop may introduce a brighter palette, newer era or higher realism than the house.

The fictional photographic treatment is sepia-black with low silver contrast, soft edge falloff and no identifiable real historical individual. Handwriting is graphite or iron-gall in irregular human baselines, never a modern script font.
